The first thing that struck us arriving from New Caledonia was that the water in Vanuatu is warmer, even down in the south. This was very welcome; beautiful as the water was in New Caledonia we wore wetsuits and were regularly a bit cold. 

## Snorkelling

We didn't know what to expect underwater in Vanuatu so were delighted when our first snorkel, in the north of Erromango, revealed lots of healthy coral and plenty of fish - including a beautiful cuttlefish. We enjoyed snorkelling in many other locations in Vanuatu - including the north of Efate, the Maskelynes and Tutuba, however the most memorable was at Craig's Cove on Ambrym. This was so different to everything we've seen before because of the volcanic black sand. The contrast of the coral set against the black sand background really made it stand out dramatically.

## Blue holes

The snorkelling was rewarding, however the blue holes were the highlight of our water-borne adventures in Vanuatu.

There are several blue holes around the country but the two that we went to were very close to each other on Santo, near the Oyster Island lagoon anchorage. 

A blue hole is a fresh water lake, up river from the sea, with absolutely crystal clear water filled by a spring - a deep blue colour, hence the name. The ones we visited had a small fee to enter, and had various slides and swings setup that made our visits a lot of fun! Some of the swings were enormous, endless entertainment for kids, big (us!) and small alike. 
  </div>
</div>

We visited the blue holes with several other family boats, taking various dinghies, paddleboards and kayaks up river. Playing together in these for a few hours really was a highlight of our adventures in Vanuatu, they were spectacularly beautiful.

## Dinghy regatta

With three inflatable sailing dinghies between the yachts, two Tiwals and our own Minicat, we decided to hold a match racing regatta for the kids. Misfit kindly provided the platform to host the event, laid a windward/leeward course, and acted as the start boat. Sharanna provided the most amazing triple layered cake I've ever seen, let alone one made on a boat - successfully feeding the crews of all 7 yachts, and I patched together some kind of race schedule. Thanks guys, a wonderful time for us all to remember - Final Straw, Freeranger, Jadamama, June, Kotare, Misfit and Zipper - sadly Emotion missed out on this get together.

The event was topped off with a fire and pot luck dinner hosted on a nearby beach by Philip and Rena. They and their family are always interested in interacting with cruisers and sharing stories. A fun evening of food and facepainting culminated in the most dangerous piñata game I've ever witnessed. The children, both locals and cruisers, were immaculately behaved, but with sticks accidentally flying out of our adult hosts' hands and sea snakes sliding up the beach to join us,[we were lucky to return to the boats with a full complement of children, no decapitations and not too much blood!

## Parting company

We have enjoyed our time cruising through Vanuatu in the company of several other family boats immensely, all with children around the same ages (including the 3 boats we'd been with in New Caledonia). We had a great time all coming and going on our own schedules at various times, often meeting up for a few days here and there along the way. Such is the way of life for cruisers that the time inevitably comes, often sooner rather than later, to say goodbye and continue on our own paths. This time came for our group of family boats in Santo, towards the north of Vanuatu, when we continued northwards (along with our friends on Jadamama) while the others returned south, either en route back to New Zealand or onwards to Australia. With lots of birthdays in September we decided to make an event of it in Oyster Island and spend a few days all together there, exploring the blue holes and hosting our own events (above). 

> see you again, at another time, in another place

One thing we have learned about this way of life is that after saying goodbye to another yacht's crew you never know when you're going to see each other again. There are several yachts that we've said goodbye to at least three times (particularly thinking of you Eleuthera and Sugar Shack!), only to end up together again shortly afterwards each time. You can guarantee that the weather will make a fool of any firm plans, so as well as being flexible in our planning we've learnt to say 'see you again, at another time, in another place'. We have made so many great friends, hailing from so many different parts of the world, if we don't bump into each other again on the water we hope to come and visit, or host at home, as many as possible in the future. This helps soften the sadness of saying goodbye, especially for the kids who in short order make great friends with others on so many different boats. They are all so resilient, they have an amazing ability to quickly make friends with all ages and bond so strongly over the experiences we are so fortunate to share out here. We all have so many memories to reminisce about in the future.
