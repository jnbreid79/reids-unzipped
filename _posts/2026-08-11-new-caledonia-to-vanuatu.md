This was a blink and you'll miss it passage and possibly our most uneventful passage to date, probably much to our returning crew's disappointment. Paul has previously come through the Panama Canal with us and sailed with us on our unforgettable passage from Tonga to New Zealand. We were really looking forward to Paul's return, and hoping that this passage would be less eventful than the previous one.

Paul flew to New Caledonia to join us for the passage to Vanuatu, and the deal this time was that we'd give him some time to actually explore the islands too. Well, that was the plan!

![Paul back onboard for another passage]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/gallery/2026-08/paul-joining-zipper-again-for-another-passage.webp)

Paul arrived in New Caledonia on Thursday and we checked out on Friday! But we knew we wanted to leave on Sunday afternoon/Monday morning and you can't check out on weekends, so Friday it was. We spent one more night in the marina, after friends in the lagoon said it was really windy and recommended we stay put. Early on Saturday morning we headed out into the lagoon to enjoy our last couple of days in New Caledonia.

## Amédée Lighthouse

We took Paul to Amédée Lighthouse, which we had sailed past on our way into New Caledonia and had visited early in our stay, only to discover the lighthouse was closed. This time it was open and we managed to make it to the top of the lighthouse. It is a picture perfect tropical island with beautiful crystal clear water. As well as seeing multiple turtles around the boat, we took Paul to snorkel on the outer reef, quite a long dinghy ride out and a very rough, wet and cold one back. The corals were great and we managed to spot an awesome octopus and one sleeping shark.

## The Passage to Vanuatu

The weather window dictated that we should catch the afternoon tide out of the Havannah Pass and sail to Port Resolution, Vanuatu. It was a gorgeous day for a sail through the lagoon and we stopped for lunch and a final snorkel at Petite Mato. New Caledonia gave us a beautiful farewell with a stunning sunset lighting up the red mud of the Prony hills. We put the gennaker away for the night and put the jib up for easy night time sailing and had a peaceful sail through the Havannah Pass which we timed well with the slacktide.

There was more wind than forecast, which always seems to be the case recently, and Zipper flew along throughout the night while the sky above was full of stunning stars and the most amazing view of the milky way.

![Stunning passage sunset between New Caledonia and Vanuatu]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/gallery/2026-08/sunset-on-passage-from-new-caledonia-to-vanuatu.webp)

A glorious day's sailing in beautiful sunshine followed our fast night and another stunning sunset and another outstanding night sky. As we neared Port Resolution in the dark we began to see the unmistakable orange glow of a volcano, what an incredible welcome to a new country.

> As we neared Port Resolution in the dark we began to see the unmistakable orange glow of a volcano, what an incredible welcome to a new country.

We'd been in touch with a boat already in Port Resolution who had told us that entry in the dark was alright and charts accurate, there were three other boats in the anchorage and they would leave their deck light on so we could anchor behind them. All very reassuring when you are entering a new anchorage at night. We got in and had our anchor down by 11pm, in time for a good night's sleep!
